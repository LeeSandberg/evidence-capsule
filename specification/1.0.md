# Evidence Capsule 1.0 alpha.2

An Evidence Capsule is a portable record of a conclusion and its support. The
format does not assert that a conclusion is true. It makes its support,
assumptions, policy decisions, origin, and revision relationship inspectable.

## Canonical payload

For alpha.2, remove the top-level `integrity`, `signatures`, and `approvals` fields,
serialize the remaining object as UTF-8 JSON with lexicographically sorted keys,
no insignificant whitespace, and `ensure_ascii=false`, then calculate SHA-256.
Prefix the lowercase hexadecimal digest with `sha256:`.

Alpha.1 verification retains its original rule: only `integrity` is removed.

Array order is significant. Producers must establish deterministic ordering
before hashing when their source store does not preserve semantic order.

## Revisions

Revision 1 uses a null or absent `previous_revision_hash`. A later revision sets
`previous_revision_hash` to the complete `integrity.content_hash` of its immediate
predecessor. A correction creates a new revision; it never mutates an already
distributed signed revision.

## Privacy

Evidence may be represented by a permission-scoped reference and content hash.
The format never requires private documents or personal information to be embedded.
A reference contains an absolute `uri`, `sha256:` content hash, media type, and
`private`, `restricted`, or `public` disclosure classification. Verification of
the capsule does not claim that unavailable referenced content was inspected.

## Signatures and approvals

Alpha.2 signatures use profile `evidence-capsule-signature/1`, algorithm
`Ed25519`, a base64 raw 32-byte public key, the exact capsule content hash, key
identifier, signing time, signer, and role. The signature covers every signature
field except `signature`, serialized canonically and prefixed by the domain
separator `evidence-capsule-signature/v1` followed by a zero byte.

Approvals use `evidence-capsule-approval/1` and the equivalent domain separator
`evidence-capsule-approval/v1`. The signed statement includes the exact content
hash, revision, decision, reviewer, note, key and signing time. Valid decisions
are `approved`, `rejected`, and `needs_changes`.

Signatures and approvals are detached attestations: they can be added without
changing the content hash. They cannot alter claims, evidence, policies, or
provenance without invalidating that hash. A valid embedded-key signature proves
key control, not that an untrusted key belongs to a claimed legal identity.

## Security status

Alpha.2 cryptographically verifies content attestations. Identity trust requires
an external key registry, certificate, organization policy, or prior trusted key
exchange. Consumers must report key-control verification and identity trust as
separate results.
